# K3: sentences read within the tolerance, against a memory that holds the
# sentences of the test. Fields are separated by " | ": what is expected, and the
# sentence. The expectation is "as said" (it fits, no deviation), "N as said" (N
# deviations, nothing to change in the reading), "N <reading>" (N deviations and
# the sentence as read), or "refused".
#
# What fits is read as said.
as said | The sky is blue.
as said | Birds fly.
as said | Is the sky blue?
as said | Tom has three apples.
as said | The sky is not green.
#
# A noun known only with a determiner before it gets that determiner back; the
# grammar alone allows "Snow is white", so memory decides. Memory knows what was
# meant, so this deviation is named but not counted against the allowance.
1 The sky is blue. | Sky is blue.
1 Is the sky blue? | Is sky blue?
1 The door is closed. | Door is closed.
1 Mary went to the kitchen. | Mary went to kitchen.
as said | Snow is white.
as said | Birds fly.
#
# A missing auxiliary verb is filled with "is", made to agree at no further cost.
1 The sky is blue. | The sky blue.
1 Birds are animals. | Birds animals.
2 The sky is blue. | Sky blue.
#
# An extra word is left out.
1 The sky is blue. | The the sky is blue.
1 The sky is blue. | The sky is is blue.
1 Birds fly. | Birds fly fly.
as said | The sky is very blue.
#
# An auxiliary verb that does not agree with its subject is read as the form that does.
1 The sky is blue. | The sky are blue.
1 Birds are animals. | Birds is animals.
1 Tom has three apples. | Tom have three apples.
1 Is the sky blue? | Are the sky blue?
#
# Two counted deviations need five words or more; a word of the wrong category
# costs two; an extra word is a cheaper reading than a wrong one.
2 The sky is blue. | Sky are blue.
2 The door is closed. | The the door are closed.

2 Is the sky blue? | Is sky are blue?
2 The sky is. | Sky is.

2 The sky is blue. | Sky is is blue.
refused | Sky is is.
